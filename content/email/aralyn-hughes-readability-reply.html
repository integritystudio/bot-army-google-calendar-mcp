Manuscript Readability Review  ·  Confidential

August 14, 2026

Hi Aralyn,

I finally got to sit down with An Unexpected Threesome — what a treat. I ran the whole thing through the readability tools publishers use to gauge how a book will sit with readers, and I couldn't wait to tell you what came back: it's all good news.

Think of these tools as a second pair of eyes that count the things a reader feels but never tallies — sentence rhythm, word choice, how often the writing asks someone to stop and work. None of it says anything about the storytelling. It's just information, and yours is the kind writers hope for.

Reading Ease

It's a genuinely easy, pleasant read.

The book scored 71 out of 100 on the classic reading-ease scale — higher means easier. Most popular memoirs land between 60 and 70, so you're actually a touch easier to read than the field. Nobody is putting this down because it made them work.

Accessibility

Anyone who picks it up can read it — and yes, that's a compliment.

Several independent formulas agree the writing sits at about a sixth-to-seventh-grade level. Before you make a face: that's exactly where the most-loved books live, and plenty of writers sweat to get there. Your conversations sound like conversations, not like prose dressed up for company.

Sentence Rhythm

The sentences move at the pace of good talk.

60,544 words, about 3,800 sentences, averaging just under 16 words each — long enough to carry a thought, short enough that nobody gets lost in the middle. For a book built on conversations, that's exactly the rhythm you want.

Vocabulary

Plain words carry the story; the distinctive ones give it flavor.

The everyday texture is simple — about 1.4 syllables per word, which is conversational English at its most natural. The tools did flag around 4,700 words as "unfamiliar," but that list is mostly names, places, and the particular language of your world — the words that make the book yours. Character, not difficulty.

One Thing to Watch

A few stretches run denser than the rest.

The two fussier formulas — the ones that fret over long, many-syllabled sentences — came back around a tenth-grade level, which just means a few passages pack more into a sentence than your easy average. If an editor ever pokes at anything, it'll be there. Truly minor.

The short version: the numbers say what the prose makes obvious — An Unexpected Threesome is warm, easy company, paced like good conversation. It sits right in the sweet spot where memoirs find their widest audience.

Don't let the jargon in the appendix below scare you — I put the raw numbers there in case you're curious, but these tools only measure the surface of writing. The part that makes a book worth reading isn't measurable, and you've got that part covered.

Warmly,

Alyshia
